A comprehensive analysis was performed on a cohort of peri- and postmenopausal Chinese women (n = 517) using metagenomics (n = 499), targeted metabolomics (n = 500), and whole-genome sequencing (n = 500) to identify microbiome-related biomarkers of bone health (metagenomes were also obtained from white American women for validation, n = 59). A GWAS was conducted. Serum short-chain fatty acids were measured, and the relative abundance of gut microbiota was characterized using KEGG functional modules associated with the gut microbiota.

Sequencing data for the Chinese cohort (whole-genome and metagenomic sequencing): https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/gsa-human, accession number HRA004900.
Metagenomic sequencing data are available at: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra, accession numbers PRJNA986283 (white American women) and PRJNA1011937 (Chinese women).

**Summary of the original article’s findings (Xu Lin et al., 2023)**

**Objective of the study:**

To identify which specific gut bacterial species and their metabolites affect bone mineral density (BMD) and osteoporosis risk in peri- and postmenopausal women.

**Main results:**

***Association between gut microbiota and BMD:***

In 517 Chinese women, a negative association was found between gut microbiota diversity (α- and β-diversity) and forearm BMD.

*Bacteroides vulgatus* was identified as one of the key species negatively associated with lumbar spine BMD (L1-L4). This result was validated in an independent cohort of 59 white US women (negative association with total hip BMD).

Short‑chain fatty acids (SCFAs):

Among the six measured SCFAs, only valeric acid (VA) was positively associated with L1‑L4 BMD.

Serum VA levels were negatively associated with the relative abundance of B. vulgatus.

***Causal inference (Mendelian randomization):***

Using whole‑genome sequencing data (500 individuals), *B. vulgatus* was shown to causally reduce serum valeric acid levels, likely by suppressing the growth of VA‑producing bacteria (*Oscillibacter valericigenes* and *Megasphaera elsdenii*).

**In vivo experiments (mice):**

Administration of *B. vulgatus* to ovariectomized mice (a model of postmenopausal osteoporosis) led to:

Decreased serum VA levels.

Increased bone resorption (elevated CTX‑I, increased osteoclast number).

Deteriorated bone microstructure (decreased BV/TV, Tb.Th).

Administration of valeric acid (in drinking water) to ovariectomized mice decreased bone resorption and improved bone microstructure (increased Tb.Th, BV/TV).

**In vitro experiments (cell cultures):**

VA (1 mM) suppressed the differentiation of RAW264.7 macrophages into osteoclast‑like cells (fewer TRAP‑positive multinucleated cells).

VA stimulated the differentiation of MC3T3‑E1 pre‑osteoblasts into osteoblasts and enhanced extracellular matrix mineralization (increased alkaline phosphatase activity and alizarin red staining).

Molecular mechanisms: VA reduced levels of phosphorylated RELA (a pro‑inflammatory NF‑κB subunit) and increased expression of anti‑inflammatory IL10, as well as p‑NFKBIA and p‑CHUK/IKBKB.

**Сonclusion:**
*B. vulgatus* reduces BMD by suppressing valeric acid production (likely through inhibiting VA‑producing bacteria). *Valeric acid*, in turn, protects bone tissue by reducing inflammation and bone resorption while promoting osteoblastogenesis. Thus, *B. vulgatus* and valeric acid may represent new targets for osteoporosis prevention and treatment.
